## Result 1

A synchronous reset is a reset signal that is sampled with respect to the clock signal. It becomes effective only at the active clock edge. The reset signal must be asserted in sync with the clock to reset the flip-flops.

**Advantages of Synchronous Reset:**

*   Ensures a fully synchronous design.
*   The Intel Quartus Prime Timing Analyzer can more efficiently analyze synchronous designs.
*   Avoids timing hazards and metastability issues that can arise with asynchronous resets.
*   By synchronizing the reset signal to the clock, designers can ensure that the reset operation occurs at a known and stable point in the system's timing, reducing the risk of unpredictable behavior.
*   Provides filtering for the reset signal, so the circuit will not be affected by glitches.
*   Ensures consistent behavior in digital circuits.
*   Simplifies circuit design, debugging, and verification processes.

**Disadvantages of Synchronous Reset:**

*   Reset needs to be stretched if it is not long enough to be seen at the active clock edge.
*   Requires the presence of a clock to reset the circuit.
*   It is slow.
*   Synthesis will not be able to easily differentiate reset from other signals.
*   May lead to timing issues.
*   If there are gated clocks for power saving, this type of reset won't be suitable.

**How it works:**

*   Synchronous reset relies on the clock signal to synchronize the reset operation with other circuit operations.
*   This ensures that the reset happens at specific moments in the clock cycle, enhancing stability and avoiding glitches.
*   When a reset signal is applied synchronously to a flip-flop, it is typically asserted during a specific phase of the clock cycle, such as the rising or falling edge.
*   This ensures that the reset operation occurs at a well-defined and predictable time, allowing the flip-flop to transition to its reset state in a controlled manner.

**Implementation**
In Verilog, a synchronous reset is implemented inside an always block that is sensitive to the clock edge:

```verilog
always @(posedge clk) begin
  if (reset)
    q <= 0;
  else
    q <= d;
end
```

**Additional considerations:**

*   Synchronous resets generally ensure that the circuit is 100% synchronous.
*   Synchronous resets ensure that the reset can only occur at an active clock edge.
*   The clock works as a filter for small reset glitches; however, if these glitches occur near the active clock edge, the flip-flop could go metastable.
*   In some designs, the reset must be generated by a set of internal conditions.
*   A synchronous reset is recommended for these types of designs because it will filter the logic equation glitches between clocks.
*   Like any broadcast signal, a synchronous reset has a high fan-out.
*   The high fan-out makes it difficult (and at times impossible) to close timing without pipelining and duplicating the synchronous reset source.
*   If you use an asynchronous reset source, synchronize the source before feeding it to the register's reset input.
*   Whether you synchronize the reset source internally or externally, it is part of the data logic path.
*   Hence, you can easily determine the data arrival and data required times for proper slack analysis.
*   A good technique for synchronous reset is to build a balanced reset tree.
*   The main goal of this design technique is to address the critical paths connecting the synchronous reset line.
*   The Fitter accomplishes this by moving the registers on the pipeline and retiming them as needed to resolve timing issues.
