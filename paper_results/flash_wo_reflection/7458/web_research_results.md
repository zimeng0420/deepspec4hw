Verilog is a hardware description language (HDL) used for modeling electronic systems. It is commonly used in the design and verification of digital circuits. Verilog supports different levels of abstraction, including behavioral, register-transfer, and gate levels.

Key aspects of Verilog include:

*   **Modules:** A Verilog design consists of a hierarchy of modules. Modules are the basic building blocks. They encapsulate the design and communicate through input, output, and bidirectional ports.
*   **Ports:** Modules communicate with other modules through declared input, output, and bidirectional ports.
*   **Data Types:** Verilog uses various data types, including `wire`, `reg`, `integer`, and `time`. `Wire` represents a physical connection, while `reg` represents a variable that stores a value.
*   **Operators:** Verilog includes logical, arithmetic, and bitwise operators.
*   **Gate Primitives:** Verilog has built-in primitives for basic logic gates like `and`, `nand`, `or`, `nor`, `xor`, and `not`.
*   **Continuous Assignment:** The `assign` statement is used for continuous assignment, which drives a value onto a wire.

The design of digital circuits in Verilog can be done at different levels:

*   **Gate Level:** The circuit is described using basic logic gates and their interconnections.
*   **Dataflow Level:** The circuit is designed in terms of data flow between registers.
*   **Behavioral Level:** The system is described by concurrent algorithms.
