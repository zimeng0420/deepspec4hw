## Result 1

A synchronous reset is a technique in digital circuit design where a flip-flop or a group of flip-flops are reset in synchronization with the clock signal. The reset signal is typically synchronized to the clock to ensure the reset operation occurs at a specific time, usually at the rising or falling edge of the clock signal. This synchronization helps to avoid timing hazards and ensures the reset operation is performed predictably and reliably within the system's clocked operation. Resets are crucial in digital circuits for ensuring the system starts from a known state and to allow the system to recover from errors by reinitializing the state.

Here's a breakdown of synchronous resets in Moore state machines:

**Concept and Working Principle**

*   **Clock Synchronization:** Synchronous resets rely on the clock signal to synchronize the reset operation with other circuit operations. The clock signal acts as a time reference, defining when the reset operation takes place.
*   **Predictable Timing:** The reset signal is asserted during a specific phase of the clock cycle, such as the rising or falling edge. This ensures the reset operation occurs at a well-defined and predictable time, allowing the flip-flop to transition to its reset state in a controlled manner.
*   **Moore Machine Consideration:** In a Moore machine, the output depends only on the current state. The state transitions, including those caused by the synchronous reset, are synchronized with the clock signal.

**Advantages of Synchronous Resets**

*   **Avoids Timing Hazards:** Synchronizing the reset signal to the clock helps avoid timing hazards and metastability issues that can arise with asynchronous resets.
*   **Improved Stability:** Synchronous reset plays a crucial role in ensuring the stability of digital circuits. By synchronizing the reset operation with the clock signal, it helps avoid glitches and erroneous behavior that may occur with asynchronous resets.
*   **Consistent Behavior:** Synchronous reset ensures consistent behavior in digital circuits by following a well-defined timing pattern, guaranteeing that the reset operation occurs at a known and predictable point in the clock cycle.
*   **Fully Synchronous Circuit:** Synchronous reset ensures the entire circuit is synchronous.
*   **Filters Glitches:** Synchronous reset ensures the reset actions occur at the clock edges, preventing the flip-flop from going into metastability. Synchronous resets provide some filtering for the reset signal such that it is not affected by glitches, unless they occur right at the clock edge.
*   **Efficient Analysis:** The use of synchronous resets helps to ensure a fully synchronous design, which the Quartus® Prime Timing Analyzer can more efficiently analyze.

**Disadvantages of Synchronous Resets**

*   **Reset Pulse Width:** The reset signal should be applied long enough so that the clock edge samples the reset signal.
*   **Need for Pulse Stretching:** If the reset signal is not long enough to be captured at the active clock edge, it will result in failure of assertion. In such a case, the design needs a pulse stretcher to guarantee that a reset pulse is wide enough to be present during the active clock edge.
*   **Clock Requirement:** Synchronous reset requires a clock to reset the circuit.
*   **Synthesis Challenges:** The logic synthesis cannot easily distinguish the reset signal from any other data signal. Proper care has to be taken with logic synthesis, else the reset signal may take the fastest path to the flip-flop input there by making worst case timing hard to meet.

**Design Strategies**

*   **Balanced Reset Tree:** A good technique for synchronous reset is to build a balanced reset tree.
*   **Internal Synchronous Resets:** A synchronous reset is recommended for some types of designs where the reset is generated by a set of internal conditions.

**Verilog Implementation**

*   **Synchronous Reset in Verilog:** To implement a synchronous reset, the reset signal is checked at the active clock edge. The reset signal must be asserted in sync with the clock to reset the flip-flops.
*   **Coding Style:** The coding style to model the reset should be an `if/else` priority style with the reset in the `if` condition and all other combinational logic in the `else` section.

With the above information, you can implement the Moore state machine with a synchronous reset in Verilog. The key is to ensure that the reset signal is only effective at the active clock edge and that the state is set to 'OFF' when the reset is active.
