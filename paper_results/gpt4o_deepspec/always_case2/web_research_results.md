## Result 1

Combinational circuits and sequential circuits are fundamental categories of digital circuits. The key distinction lies in how they generate outputs.

**Combinational Circuits:**

*   A combinational circuit's output depends solely on the current input values at that instant in time. Past inputs or previous states have no bearing on the output.
*   They do not have memory elements.
*   Combinational circuits are memoryless. They don't store any prior state.
*   They are generally faster and simpler in design.
*   They are considered open-loop systems because they lack feedback paths between input and output.
*   Examples of combinational circuits include adders, subtractors, multiplexers, decoders.
*   Combinational circuits are used for arithmetic and logic units (ALUs), data encryption/decryption, data multiplexing/demultiplexing.
*   The basic building blocks are logic gates.
*   They produce an instantaneous output in response to input changes.
*   They are time-independent.
*   They exhibit deterministic behavior, generating predictable and repeatable results based on the present input.

**Sequential Circuits:**

*   A sequential circuit's output depends on both the present inputs and the past history of the circuit.
*   They incorporate memory elements (flip-flops or latches) to store information about past inputs and previous states.
*   They have a feedback path.
*   Sequential circuits can process streams of input data over a period of time.
*   They are used to implement state machines, timers, counters, and memory elements.
*   They are more complex due to memory and clock management.
*   Examples include counters, registers, and finite state machines.
*   A clock signal is often used to synchronize operations.
*   They are time-dependent systems.
*   They are essential for operations requiring memory and time-based control.
*   They can be synchronous or asynchronous. Synchronous circuits use a clock signal.
*   They are commonly modeled in Verilog using "always" blocks.
